Exercise: Prokaryotic regulation (recap)

Goal

By the end of this exercise you should be able to:

  • predict β-galactosidase and permease expression (inducible, constitutive or none) for haploid and merodiploid lac strains;
  • use dominance and cis vs trans to decide whether a mutation changes a protein or a DNA site;
  • design a merodiploid that tells two mutations with the same haploid phenotype apart;
  • find the lac genes in NCBI Gene and read their position and strand on the E. coli chromosome.

Background

This is a recap of 5MO103, seen through genetics. The lac operon has a promoter (P), an operator (O) and three genes transcribed as one mRNA: lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease) and lacA. The repressor gene lacI has its own promoter and is expressed all the time. Repressor binds O and blocks transcription; the inducer (allolactose, or IPTG in the lab) binds the repressor and releases it.

Alleles.

Allele Defect Haploid phenotype
lacI− no working repressor constitutive
lacIs repressor cannot bind inducer (super-repressor) uninducible
lacOc operator cannot bind repressor constitutive
lacZ− no active β-galactosidase no β-gal; permease normal
lacY− no active permease no permease; β-gal normal

Merodiploids carry a second lac region on an F′ plasmid. We write F′ genotype / chromosome genotype; genes not mentioned are wild type.

The logic. Repressor diffuses, so lacI alleles act in trans on both copies: I+ is dominant over I−, and Is is dominant over I+. The operator is a DNA site, so lacO alleles act only in cis on the genes of their own copy. To predict a merodiploid: (1) decide which repressor is in the cell; (2) for each copy, decide whether its operator is free; (3) a copy that is transcribed makes β-gal if it is Z+ and permease if it is Y+.

Simplifications. The inducer reaches the repressor whatever the lacZ/lacY genotypes (as with IPTG). We ignore CAP–cAMP (glucose) and leaky basal expression.

By hand

B1. (haploids) For each haploid strain, give β-galactosidase and permease as inducible, constitutive or none: a) I+ O+ Z+ Y+; b) I− Z−; c) Is; d) Oc Y−; e) Is Oc.

B2. (merodiploids) Do the same for:

  1. F′ I+ Z− / I− Z+
  2. F′ Is Z− / I+ Z+
  3. F′ Oc Z− Y+ / O+ Z+ Y−
  4. F′ Oc Z+ Y− / Is Z− Y+
  5. F′ I− Oc Z+ / I− O+ Z−

B3. (I− or Oc?) You have isolated a new constitutive mutant, c1, on the chromosome. It is Z+. You have an F′ that is I+ O+ Z− Y+. Put the F′ into the mutant and measure β-galactosidase. What result do you expect if c1 is in lacI? If it is in lacO?

B4. (an uninducible mutant) A new mutant, u1, makes neither β-gal nor permease, with or without lactose. Two explanations: u1 is an Is allele, or it is a promoter mutation (P−) that RNA polymerase cannot use.

  1. Predict β-gal in F′ I+ P+ O+ Z+ / u1 for each explanation.
  2. Which of the two would you call cis-acting?

B5. (complementation) Three lac− mutants cannot grow on lactose. m1 and m3 lack β-galactosidase; m2 lacks permease. All have normal regulation. Merodiploids give:

F′ / chromosome grows on lactose?
m1 / m2 yes
m1 / m3 no
m2 / m3 yes

How many genes do the mutations define? Which are in the same gene? Why must you be careful if one of the mutations is a nonsense mutation near the start of lacZ?

B6. (attenuation) In the trp leader peptide, the two Trp codons are changed by mutation to codons for alanine. Will the trp operon now respond to tryptophan starvation through attenuation? To which starvation will attenuation respond?

Explore

Do the by-hand part first. In the demo, set the chromosome and the F′ genotypes with the buttons (or “no F′” for a haploid), and switch lactose on and off. The demo draws the repressor on each operator, the mRNA, and the proteins made from each copy, then sums up the phenotype for both inducer states and explains it. The presets are classic strains.

E1. Click the presets “I⁻” and then “F′ I⁺ / I⁻”. What changes in the drawing between the two, and why is this called acting in trans?

E2. Click “F′ O⁺ Z⁻ / Oᶜ Z⁺” and then “F′ O⁺ Z⁺ / Oᶜ Z⁻”. Compare the β-galactosidase phenotypes. Which copy makes the active enzyme in each case?

E3. Click “F′ I⁺ / Iˢ” and toggle lactose. Then, keeping the chromosome Is, change the F′ operator to c. Which copy is now transcribed, and why can the super-repressor not stop it?

E4. Set up your answers to B2 c) and d) in the demo and check them.

E5. Click “F′ Z⁺ Y⁻ / Z⁻ Y⁺”. Which copy makes β-gal and which makes permease? Is the strain able to use lactose?

Solutions: Explore

E1. In the I− haploid there is no repressor, so the operator is free and the operon is transcribed with or without lactose: both enzymes constitutive. Adding F′ I+ puts normal repressor into the cell, and it sits on both operators, the F′ one and the chromosomal one, until lactose is added: both enzymes become inducible. The repressor made from one DNA molecule acts on the other, which is what trans means.

E2. F′ O+ Z− / Oc Z+: β-gal constitutive, made from the chromosome, whose Oc operator never binds repressor. F′ O+ Z+ / Oc Z−: β-gal inducible; the chromosome is still transcribed all the time, but its β-gal is inactive (Z⁻), and active β-gal comes only from the F′, which is repressed until lactose is added. In both strains, permease is constitutive (the Oc copy is Y+ and always transcribed).

E3. F′ I+ / Is: the purple super-repressor sits on both operators, with and without lactose: no β-gal and no permease. Inducer binds the normal repressor (drawn with an orange diamond) but not Is. With F′ Oc, the F′ copy is transcribed all the time: both enzymes constitutive. Oc is a change in the DNA that the repressor binds; a repressor of any kind cannot bind it.

E4. c) β-gal inducible (from the chromosome, O+ Z+), permease constitutive (from the F′, Oc Y+). d) β-gal constitutive (from the F′, Oc Z+), permease none (the only Y+ is on the chromosome, whose O+ is blocked by the Is repressor).

E5. β-gal from the F′ (Z+), permease from the chromosome (Y+), both inducible. The cell has both proteins, so it can use lactose: the two mutations complement.

Find it

NCBI Gene has one entry per gene and organism. For E. coli, use the reference strain K-12 MG1655, whose chromosome is the RefSeq sequence NC_000913.3. In a gene page, the Genomic context section gives the location as a range on that sequence; “complement” means that the gene is on the minus strand, read from right to left.

F1. Open NCBI Gene for lacZ in E. coli K-12. In the Summary, read the Gene description. In Genomic context, read the Location. Is lacZ on the plus or the minus strand?

F2. Open NCBI Gene for lacI and read its Location the same way. Using F1 and F2, draw the order of lacI and lacZ along the chromosome and show the direction in which each is transcribed. Is lacI upstream or downstream of lacZ? Does lacI belong to the lac operon mRNA?

F3. On the lacZ and lacI pages, scroll to NCBI Reference Sequences (RefSeq), protein (the NP_ accession). How many amino acids long are β-galactosidase and the repressor? (The protein pages say this as “aa” in the first line.) Alternatively, use EcoCyc, the curated E. coli database, for lacI.

Hint

A gene on the minus strand at complement(a..b) starts at b (its 5′ end) and ends at a. A gene “upstream” of lacZ lies before the lacZ start in the direction of lacZ transcription.

Solutions

Solutions: By hand

B1. a) wild type: both inducible. b) I− Z−: β-gal none (no working lacZ), permease constitutive. c) Is: both none (uninducible). d) Oc Y−: β-gal constitutive, permease none. e) Is Oc: both constitutive; the super-repressor cannot bind an Oc operator.

B2. a) β-gal inducible: the only Z+ is on the chromosome, and the F′ I+ repressor reaches that operator in trans. Permease inducible. b) both none: Is on the F′ is trans-dominant and blocks the chromosome’s operator. c) β-gal inducible (chromosome O+ Z+), permease constitutive (F′ Oc Y+). d) β-gal constitutive (F′ Oc Z+), permease none (the chromosome’s Y+ is behind an O+ blocked by Is). e) No working repressor at all (both I−). β-gal constitutive (F′ Z+); permease constitutive (both copies Y+ and transcribed).

B3. If c1 is lacI−: the F′ supplies I+ repressor in trans, which controls the chromosome’s O+ Z+ → β-gal inducible (c1 is recessive). If c1 is lacOc: the chromosome’s Z+ sits next to an operator that repressor cannot bind, whatever the F′ provides → β-gal constitutive (c1 is cis-dominant). The F′ must be Z− so that all active β-gal comes from the chromosome; otherwise the F′ copy’s own inducible β-gal would muddy the result.

B4. a) If u1 is Is: the super-repressor blocks the F′ operator too → none (dominant, trans). If u1 is P−: the F′ copy is normal and regulated by the normal repressor → inducible (u1 recessive; it only fails to transcribe its own copy). b) P− is cis-acting: a promoter is a DNA site that only drives the genes next to it.

B5. m1 and m3 fail to complement, so they are in the same gene (lacZ, since both lack β-gal). m2 complements both, so it is in a different gene (lacY). Two genes. Caution: a polar nonsense mutation early in lacZ reduces expression of the downstream lacY from the same mRNA. Such an allele can fail to complement a lacY mutation even though the lacY gene on that copy is intact, which would wrongly suggest that the two are in the same gene.

B6. No. Attenuation reads tryptophan through ribosome stalling at the Trp codons. Without Trp codons in the leader peptide, tryptophan starvation no longer stalls the ribosome there, so the 3:4 terminator forms as usual (the repressor still responds to tryptophan). Instead, alanine starvation would now stall the ribosome in region 1 and allow read-through. (Leader peptides of other amino-acid operons use exactly this trick: the his leader has seven His codons in a row.)

Solutions: Find it

F1. Gene description: beta-galactosidase. Location: NC_000913.3 (363231..366305, complement), on the minus strand. Click path: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/945006 → Summary → Gene description; Genomic context → Location.

F2. lacI: NC_000913.3 (366428..367510, complement), also on the minus strand. Both genes are read from higher to lower coordinates, so the order in the direction of transcription is lacI (366428–367510) → lacZ (363231–366305) → lacY → lacA. lacI lies upstream of lacZ, right next to the operon, but it has its own promoter and its own mRNA; it is not part of the lacZYA transcript. (That the repressor gene sits next to its target is a convenience of evolution, not a requirement: as the merodiploids show, the repressor works in trans from anywhere in the cell.)

F3. β-galactosidase (NP_414878) is 1024 aa; the lac repressor (NP_414879) is 360 aa. Click path: gene page → NCBI Reference Sequences (RefSeq) → mRNA and Protein(s) → the NP_ link → first line of the protein record. (EcoCyc gives the same lengths on its lacI and lacZ pages, IDs EG10525 and EG10527.)

Where this leads

The same reasoning works in eukaryotes. An enhancer is a cis-acting DNA site, like lacO; a transcription factor is a trans-acting protein, like LacI. In Eukaryotic regulation & epigenetics we add chromatin, DNA methylation and X inactivation, which bacteria do not have.
