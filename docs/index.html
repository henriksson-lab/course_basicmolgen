Basic and molecular genetics

Course material for Basic and molecular genetics (5MO104, 15 ECTS, Umeå University). The material follows the course book, Hartwell et al., Genetics: From Genes to Genomes. On top of the genetics, every unit trains you to find information in public databases and to analyse it in R.

Where to start

  • Learning outcomes: what we are trying to teach.
  • Install R and the packages you need: do this before the first exercise.
  • Glossary: the terms used in the course.

How each unit works

Work through the sidebar from top to bottom. Each unit has:

  • a lecture (slides, in green);
  • a quiz (in blue) to check that you understood the concepts;
  • an exercise, in this order:
    • By hand: genetics problems on paper.
    • Explore: an interactive demo (in orange).
    • Find it: look up real genes, diseases and variants in public databases.
  • an exercise in R: a small analysis or simulation that builds on the exercise.

Topics

  • Part 1: Mendelian genetics. Genes and alleles, Mendel’s laws, probability and the χ² test, extensions to Mendel (dominance, lethality, epistasis, penetrance), and pedigree analysis.
  • Part 2: Chromosomes and mapping. Meiosis, sex linkage and nondisjunction; linkage and recombination; three-point crosses; human linkage and LOD scores.
  • Part 3: DNA, mutation and gene function. DNA and replication (recap), mutation and complementation, and gene expression (recap).
  • Part 4: Genome analysis. Restriction enzymes, PCR and sequencing; genome annotation and BLAST; genomic variation across populations.
  • Part 5: Chromosomes in detail. Chromatin, centromeres and telomeres; rearrangements and aneuploidy; bacterial genetics (recap); mitochondrial and chloroplast inheritance.
  • Part 6: Gene regulation and development. The lac operon as genetic analysis; eukaryotic regulation, epigenetics and X inactivation; RNA-mediated regulation; development and model organisms.
  • Part 7: Cancer, populations and complex traits. Cancer genetics; Hardy–Weinberg; drift and selection; heritability and GWAS.
  • Ethics. Genetic testing, privacy, germline editing, and ethical review of research.
