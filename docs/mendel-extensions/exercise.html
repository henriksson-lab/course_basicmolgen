Exercise: extensions to Mendel

Goal

By the end of this exercise you should be able to:

  • predict F₁ and F₂ phenotypes with incomplete dominance, codominance and multiple alleles (ABO, an allelic series);
  • explain at the molecular level why an allele is dominant or recessive;
  • recognise a recessive lethal from a 2 : 1 ratio, and use penetrance in a probability calculation;
  • derive the modified dihybrid ratios 9 : 3 : 4, 9 : 7, 12 : 3 : 1, 13 : 3 and 15 : 1 from a pathway and a Punnett square;
  • turn an F₂ into 16ths, pick candidate ratios, and test them with χ²;
  • look up a gene’s location in NCBI Gene and a disease’s mode of inheritance in MedGen/OMIM.

Background

One gene.

  • Complete dominance: the heterozygote looks like one homozygote; F₂ 3 : 1.
  • Incomplete dominance: the heterozygote is intermediate; F₂ phenotypes 1 : 2 : 1 (snapdragons: red, pink, white).
  • Codominance: the heterozygote shows both phenotypes; IAIB is blood type AB.
  • Multiple alleles: IA, IB, i give 6 genotypes and 4 blood types. Rabbit coat: C > cch > ch > c.
  • Recessive lethal: a 1 : 2 : 1 with the homozygous class dead gives 2 : 1 among survivors (yellow mice, AY).
  • Penetrance: P(phenotype | genotype). Expressivity: how strongly the phenotype shows.

Two genes. Start from the dihybrid F₂, 9 A_B_ : 3 A_bb : 3 aaB_ : 1 aabb, and merge the classes that look the same:

F₂ ratio Name Merged classes Test cross AaBb × aabb
9 : 3 : 4 recessive epistasis aaB_ + aabb 1 : 1 : 2
9 : 7 complementary genes A_bb + aaB_ + aabb 1 : 3
12 : 3 : 1 dominant epistasis A_B_ + A_bb 2 : 1 : 1
13 : 3 dominant suppression A_B_ + A_bb + aabb 3 : 1
15 : 1 duplicate genes A_B_ + A_bb + aaB_ 3 : 1

χ² goodness of fit (from Probability and the χ² test): \(\chi^2 = \sum (O - E)^2 / E\), with df = number of classes − 1. The critical value at α = 0.05 is 3.84 for df = 1, 5.99 for df = 2 and 7.81 for df = 3.

By hand

B1. (incomplete dominance) In snapdragons, CRCR is red, CRCW pink and CWCW white. Give the phenotype ratios from (a) pink × pink, (b) pink × white, (c) red × white. Which of the three crosses would you use to get the most pink seed?

B2. (ABO) A woman with blood type AB has a child with a man of blood type O. (a) Which blood types are possible for the child, and in what proportions? (b) Could this couple have a child with type O? With type AB?

B3. (allelic series) In rabbits, C (full colour) > cch (chinchilla) > ch (Himalayan) > c (albino). A chinchilla rabbit crossed with a Himalayan rabbit gives 1/2 chinchilla, 1/4 Himalayan and 1/4 albino offspring. What are the genotypes of the two parents?

B4. (lethal allele) Yellow mice (AYa) are crossed with each other, and give litters with on average 8 live pups. (a) What fraction of the zygotes die? (b) Among the live pups, what fraction are yellow? (c) If each litter of yellow × agouti also starts from the same number of zygotes, which cross gives larger litters, and by how much?

B5. (penetrance) Polydactyly in a family is inherited as an autosomal dominant trait with 70% penetrance. A man without extra digits has an affected father and an unaffected mother who does not carry the allele. His partner does not carry the allele. (a) What is the probability that the man carries the allele? (Hint: use the fact that he is unaffected.) (b) What is the probability that their first child has extra digits?

B6. (recessive epistasis) In Labradors, B_ gives black and bb brown (chocolate) pigment, but ee dogs are yellow whatever their B genotype. (a) Give the F₂ phenotype ratio from BbEe × BbEe, and explain it with a pathway. (b) A black dog is crossed with a yellow bbee dog. Their pups are 3 black, 2 chocolate and 5 yellow. What is the genotype of the black dog? (c) Can two yellow Labradors have a black pup? Explain.

B7. (complementary genes) Two pure-breeding white-flowered sweet pea lines, W1 and W2, are crossed. All F₁ plants are purple, and the F₂ has 9 purple : 7 white. (a) Give the genotypes of W1, W2 and the F₁. (b) What fraction of the white F₂ plants would breed true if selfed (give only white offspring)? (c) A third white line, W3, crossed with W1 gives only white F₁ plants. What does that tell you about the mutation in W3?

B8. (identify the ratio) Summer squash plants from a dihybrid cross give an F₂ of 205 white, 52 yellow and 15 green fruit. (a) Express the counts in 16ths and suggest a ratio. (b) Test it with χ² by hand. (c) Draw a pathway that explains the ratio, and say which genotypes give green fruit.

B9. (dominant suppression and duplicate genes) (a) White Leghorn chickens (CCII) are crossed with White Wyandottes (ccii). C is needed for coloured feathers, and I suppresses colour. What are the F₁ and F₂ phenotypes? (b) A dihybrid F₂ gives 15 : 1 for triangular : ovoid seed capsules. What ratio would an F₁ test cross give, and why can a test cross not separate 15 : 1 from 13 : 3?

Explore

The demo shows the 16 squares of AaBb × AaBb coloured by phenotype, next to the pathway that explains the colours. Click a square to see which enzymes that genotype has and how far down the pathway it gets.

E1. In recessive epistasis 9 : 3 : 4, click aaBB, then aabb. Both are yellow. Which enzyme is missing in each, and why does the B genotype not matter? Which class of 16ths has merged with which?

E2. Switch to complementary 9 : 7. Click AAbb and aaBB (the two white parents) and then AaBb (their F₁). Explain why two white parents can give a purple F₁. How many different genotypes are white?

E3. Compare dominant epistasis 12 : 3 : 1 with dominant suppression 13 : 3. In both, A_ switches an inhibitor on. Click aabb in each. Why is aabb a class of its own in one preset and merged with white in the other?

E4. Go through all six presets and, for each one, write down which of the four classes A_B_, A_bb, aaB_, aabb are merged. Check your answers against the table in the Background.

E5. In duplicate genes 15 : 1, how many of the 16 squares have no working enzyme? How many have exactly one kind of working enzyme? Why would a geneticist who knocked out only gene A see no phenotype?

Solutions

E1. aaBB lacks enzyme A, so the yellow precursor is never turned into brown, and enzyme B has nothing to work on. aabb lacks both enzymes. Both stop at yellow. The B genotype only matters if step A happens, so aa hides B/b: aa is epistatic. The 3 aaB_ and the 1 aabb merge into 4.

E2. AAbb has enzyme A but not B; aaBB has B but not A. Both are white. The F₁ AaBb gets a working A from one parent and a working B from the other, so both steps work and it is purple: the two mutations complement each other. White squares: AAbb, Aabb, aaBB, aaBb, aabb, which are 5 genotypes in 7 of the 16 squares.

E3. In dominant epistasis, the inhibitor blocks the first step (white → green). aabb has no inhibitor, so it makes green, but it has no enzyme B to make yellow: it stays green, a class of its own (1/16). In dominant suppression, the pathway has only one step, done by enzyme B. aabb has no inhibitor but also no enzyme B, so it is colourless, like the inhibited plants: 9 + 3 + 1 = 13.

E4. None merged: 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. Recessive epistasis: aaB_ + aabb. Complementary: A_bb + aaB_ + aabb. Dominant epistasis: A_B_ + A_bb. Dominant suppression: A_B_ + A_bb + aabb. Duplicate: A_B_ + A_bb + aaB_.

E5. Only 1 square (aabb) has no working enzyme. Squares with only enzyme A are the 3 A_bb, and squares with only enzyme B are the 3 aaB_: 6 squares have exactly one kind. An aa knockout still has enzyme B (if it is B_), which makes the product on its own. Redundant genes have to be knocked out together before a phenotype appears.

Find it

F1. The ABO gene. Open NCBI Gene and search for ABO[sym] AND human[orgn]. Open the ABO entry. In the Summary box, read the Location field and the full gene name. On which chromosome is ABO, and in which cytogenetic band? What kind of enzyme does the gene encode, according to its name?

F2. A disease with incomplete penetrance. Open MedGen and search for hereditary breast ovarian cancer syndrome. Open the entry with that name. Read the Mode of inheritance field. Then open the BRCA1 gene in NCBI Gene and read Location. The OMIM entry for the BRCA1-related disease is MIM 604370 (Breast-ovarian cancer, familial, susceptibility to, 1). Does everyone who inherits a pathogenic BRCA1 variant get cancer? What word from the lecture describes this?

F3. Achondroplasia. Open the MedlinePlus Genetics page on achondroplasia and read the Causes and Inheritance sections. Then check the mode of inheritance in MedGen (entry Achondroplasia). OMIM: MIM 100800. (a) What is the mode of inheritance, and which gene is involved? (b) About what fraction of people with achondroplasia have parents of average height? What does that tell you about where their allele came from? (c) What happens to a child who inherits two altered copies? Which idea from the lecture is this? (d) Is the altered protein less active or more active than normal? Which molecular kind of dominance is it?

F4. Where is FGFR3? In NCBI Gene, read the Location of human FGFR3. Is it on the same chromosome as ABO?

Solutions

F1. NCBI Gene → search ABO[sym] AND human[orgn] → ABO (Gene ID 28) → Summary. Location: 9q34.2, so chromosome 9, long arm (q), band 34.2. The full name is “ABO, alpha 1-3-N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase and alpha 1-3-galactosyltransferase”: a glycosyltransferase. The A allele adds N-acetylgalactosamine and the B allele galactose to the H antigen, which is why A and B are codominant.

F2. MedGen → Hereditary breast ovarian cancer syndrome (concept C0677776) → Mode of inheritance: autosomal dominant inheritance. NCBI Gene BRCA1 (Gene ID 672): Location 17q21.31. Not everyone who carries a pathogenic BRCA1 variant develops cancer: they inherit a raised risk, not the disease itself. This is incomplete penetrance. (We do not ask for a penetrance number, because the estimates differ between studies and change over time.)

F3. (a) Autosomal dominant; gene FGFR3 (MedlinePlus Inheritance section; MedGen Achondroplasia, C0001080: “Autosomal dominant inheritance”). (b) About 80% of people with achondroplasia are born to parents of average height. Their FGFR3 allele is a new (de novo) mutation, usually arising in a parent’s germ line. (c) Children with two altered copies have a severe form, with very short bones and a small rib cage; they are usually stillborn or die soon after birth. The allele is dominant for short stature and recessive lethal, like AY in mice. (d) MedlinePlus: the variants “cause the FGFR3 protein to be overly active”. This is a gain-of-function allele.

F4. NCBI Gene → FGFR3 (Gene ID 2261) → Location 4p16.3: chromosome 4, short arm. Not the same chromosome as ABO (9q34.2).

Solutions

Solutions: By hand

B1. (a) Pink × pink (CRCW × CRCW): 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white. (b) Pink × white (CRCW × CWCW): 1 pink : 1 white. (c) Red × white (CRCR × CWCW): all pink. Cross (c) gives the most pink seed (100%), and since pink plants do not breed true you must make this cross every season.

B2. The mother is IAIB and the father ii. (a) The child gets IA or IB from the mother (1/2 each) and i from the father: 1/2 type A (IAi) and 1/2 type B (IBi). (b) Neither O nor AB is possible: every child gets exactly one of IA or IB from the mother, and i from the father.

B3. Albino offspring (cc) mean both parents carry c. A chinchilla parent cchc × a Himalayan parent chc gives 1/4 cchch (chinchilla), 1/4 cchc (chinchilla), 1/4 chc (Himalayan), 1/4 cc (albino) = 1/2 chinchilla : 1/4 Himalayan : 1/4 albino. The parents are cchc and chc.

B4. (a) AYa × AYa gives 1/4 AYAY, which die: 1/4 of the zygotes. (b) The survivors are 2 AYa : 1 aa: 2/3 yellow. (c) Yellow × agouti (AYa × aa) gives 1/2 AYa and 1/2 aa, with no deaths. If 8 live pups are 3/4 of the zygotes, a litter starts from about 8 / (3/4) ≈ 10.7 zygotes, so yellow × agouti litters are about 4/3 times as large (≈ 10.7 pups instead of 8).

B5. (a) Before we know his phenotype, P(carrier) = 1/2 (affected father is heterozygous; mother is +/+). Then use the fact that he is unaffected: P(unaffected and carrier) = 1/2 × 0.3 = 0.15; P(unaffected and not carrier) = 1/2 × 1 = 0.5. P(carrier | unaffected) = 0.15 / (0.15 + 0.5) = 0.15 / 0.65 ≈ 0.23. (b) P(child affected) = P(man carrier) × P(passes it on) × penetrance = 0.23 × 1/2 × 0.7 ≈ 0.081, about 8%.

B6. (a) 9 black (B_E_) : 3 chocolate (bbE_) : 4 yellow (B_ee + bbee). Pathway: E_ is needed to put dark pigment in the hair at all; B_ makes that dark pigment black rather than brown. ee stops the pathway before B matters, so ee is epistatic to B. (b) Chocolate pups (bbE_) mean the black parent carries b; yellow pups (ee) mean it carries e. So the black dog is BbEe, and this is a test cross: expected 1 black : 1 chocolate : 2 yellow, close to 3 : 2 : 5 in a litter of 10. (c) No. Yellow dogs are ee, so all their pups are ee and yellow (whatever their B genotype; their noses may differ).

B7. (a) W1 = AAbb, W2 = aaBB (or the other way round), F₁ = AaBb. (b) White F₂ plants: 1 AAbb, 2 Aabb, 1 aaBB, 2 aaBb, 1 aabb (7/16). Every one of them lacks a working copy of at least one gene for good: A_bb plants are bb, so all their offspring are bb and white; aaB_ plants are aa, so all offspring are white; aabb likewise. So all 7/7 white F₂ plants breed true for white. (c) W3 × W1 gives a white F₁, so W3 does not complement W1: the W3 mutation is in the same gene as W1’s (gene B if W1 is AAbb).

B8. (a) Total = 272. In 16ths: 205/272 × 16 = 12.06, 52/272 × 16 = 3.06, 15/272 × 16 = 0.88. Suggests 12 : 3 : 1 (dominant epistasis). (b) Expected: 272 × 12/16 = 204, 272 × 3/16 = 51, 272 × 1/16 = 17. χ² = (205 − 204)²/204 + (52 − 51)²/51 + (15 − 17)²/17 = 0.0049 + 0.0196 + 0.2353 = 0.26, df = 2. Since 0.26 < 5.99, do not reject; p ≈ 0.88. (For comparison, 9 : 3 : 4 in the same order gives χ² ≈ 46.8, clearly rejected.) (c) White precursor → (step 1) → green → (enzyme from Y) → yellow, and the dominant W allele blocks step 1. W_ is white; wwY_ is yellow; wwyy is green.

B9. (a) F₁ CcIi: white (I suppresses colour). F₂: coloured only if C_ii (3/16); C_I_ (9), ccI_ (3) and ccii (1) are white: 13 white : 3 coloured. (b) F₁ test cross AaBb × aabb gives AaBb, Aabb, aaBb, aabb in 1 : 1 : 1 : 1. Under 15 : 1 only aabb lacks the product: 3 triangular : 1 ovoid. Under 13 : 3 (coloured only aaBb) the test cross is also 3 : 1. Both merge three of the four test-cross classes, so the test cross cannot tell them apart; only the F₂ (15 : 1 vs 13 : 3) can.

Where this leads

Incomplete penetrance and de novo mutations (as in achondroplasia) come back in Pedigree analysis, where they make human family trees harder to read. The 9 : 7 cross of two white lines is the logic of the complementation test in Mutation. And when a dihybrid F₂ is not 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 because the genes sit close together on a chromosome, that is linkage, which starts Part 2.
